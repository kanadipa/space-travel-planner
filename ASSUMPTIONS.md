# Assumptions

The brief is unspecific by design, so this file records what was taken as given,
what was interpreted where the text allowed more than one reading, and what was
added outright. Every interpretation carries a reason.

## Stated

Taken directly from the prerequisites.

| # | Assumption |
|---|---|
| 3a | Planets are solid — a trajectory cannot pass through one. |
| 3b | Planets are perfect spheres. |
| 3c | Distance from the sun is measured centre to centre. |
| 4a | Spacecraft travel at constant speed. |
| 4b | Range is consumed at `R = 1 + 0.042 × n_p` per km travelled. |
| 5a | The initial departure from Earth is from the point closest to the destination. |
| 5b | A planet counts as visited on reaching any point of its surface. |
| 5c | Departure is always from the same spot as arrival. |
| 6a | Gravity and other external forces are negligible. |
| 6b | Any planet lying directly in the path must be passed around. |
| 6c | Moons do not affect the trajectory. |
| 6d | The trajectory is optimised for maximum efficiency. |
| — | Planetary motion is halted for an estimated 42 years; all bodies are collinear. |

## Interpreted

Where the brief permits more than one reading.

**Distance is one-dimensional, not geospatial.** With orbits halted and every body
on a single axis, the supplied `distance_from_sun_km` values fully determine the
geometry. Distance between two bodies is the difference of those values. No
coordinates, angles or orbital mechanics are required, and none are supplied.

**Surface-to-surface, so both radii come off.** 5a places departure at the surface
point nearest the destination and 5b places arrival at the far body's surface.
Both points sit one radius closer than the respective centres, so the travelled
distance is `|d₁ − d₂| − r₁ − r₂`. The subtraction is direction independent: the
near-facing surfaces are always the closest pair, whether travelling outward or
inward.

**A detour is a semicircle of length `πr`.** 3a forbids passing through a body and
6b requires passing around it. Of the paths that clear a sphere, the shortest
runs over its surface, which 6d selects. This replaces the `2r` a straight line
would have covered.

**Detours apply at continued-through stops but not at turnarounds.** By 5c a craft
leaves from its exact arrival point. A craft that lands and then continues in the
same direction still has the body between it and the next target, so it pays the
detour. A craft that turns around leaves the way it came and pays nothing.

**Temperature binds everywhere on the route, not only at destinations.** A body
flown around in transit is still encountered. One planet outside a craft's
operational range anywhere on the path disqualifies that craft for the whole
mission. Radiation and gravity have no stated rule attached and are treated as
descriptive.

**"Efficiency" in 6d scopes to the trajectory, not the fleet.** The word attaches
to *trajectory* in the text, and nothing in the brief concerns seat utilisation.
The chosen path is therefore the shortest that satisfies the constraints; fleet
utilisation is explicitly out of scope. See the design notes on why filling craft
to capacity would in fact reduce reach.

**Ordering multiple destinations does not change total distance.** A round trip
along one axis covers twice the span on each side of the departure point whatever
order the stops take, so no route-ordering search is required. Stops are swept
outward then inward because it reads clearly on a timeline, not because it is
uniquely optimal.

**The sun is not offered as a destination.** The supplied data lists it as a body
with `type: Star` at 5,505 °C. No craft in the fleet has an operational range
within two thousand degrees of that, so it can never be visited. It is filtered
out of the destination picker rather than shown and rejected, since offering a
choice that can never succeed is noise. The filter is data-driven — a body is
excluded when *no* craft in the fleet can survive it — rather than keyed on
`type`, so a future body with the same problem is handled without a code change.
The per-craft temperature check is unaffected and still binds at every body on a
route.

**Earth is the only origin.** Prerequisite 5a fixes the initial departure to
Earth. Mars is habitable and could restock a craft, which would make
Mars-originating missions coherent, but nothing in the brief asks for them and
supporting multiple origins would require modelling where each craft currently
sits. Out of scope for the prototype.

**A mission plan is one craft, one party, one route.** The required capabilities
are singular throughout. Bookings are not pooled onto shared flights.

**Computed values are stored, not recomputed on load.** A saved plan is a record
of what was agreed. Reopening one shows the figures the agent saw when they saved
it, not figures regenerated by whatever the code does today.

## Invented

Not in the brief at all.

**The mission window is 42 years, measured from the departure date.** The brief
gives the halted-orbit period but does not explicitly bind missions to it. Since
the straight-line model only holds while orbits are halted, a mission finishing
outside that period would be computed under a model that no longer applies.

**"Today" is the same calendar date in 2041.** The data carries no epoch, and
multi-year missions need a clock to sit on. Chosen so that a full 42-year window
is available from the default departure date.

**Stop duration is not modelled.** A realistic layover — days or weeks — is
immaterial against legs measured in years and a 42-year ceiling; it never changes
whether a mission is feasible. Modelling it would add a magic number and change
no outcome.

**A party is not split across multiple craft.** If a group exceeds a craft's
capacity, that craft is excluded rather than the party divided. Splitting a
booking is a product decision the brief does not authorise.

## Known simplifications

Deliberate fidelity limits, recorded because they are visible on inspection.

**A craft can be booked for overlapping missions.** Saving a plan does not check
whether that spacecraft is already committed on those dates, so two saved missions
can both claim the same craft in the same window. The brief asks for plans to be
saved and reloaded, not for a fleet schedule, and enforcing it properly means
deciding what a conflict blocks — the save, or only the recommendation — and how an
agent resolves one. Recorded rather than half-built.

**The trajectory model is one-dimensional.** In reality a craft that detours around
one body leaves the axis, and the return leg could pass clear of bodies it had to
avoid on the way out. Modelling that requires a two-dimensional path solver. On
the supplied data the total effect is well under one percent of trip distance —
Jupiter's radius is roughly 0.01% of the Earth–Jupiter gap — and it never changes
a feasibility outcome. The brief states the scenario is not intended to be
scientifically accurate.

**Craft attributes are snapshotted onto saved missions.** If the source data
changes, previously saved plans keep the figures they were created with.

**The 42-year window never binds on the supplied data.** The widest possible round
trip from Earth — out to Neptune and in to Mercury — is roughly 8.99 billion km,
which the slowest craft in the fleet covers in about 2.6 years. The check is
implemented because the constraint is stated, and it is verified by test, but no
selectable route can trigger it.
